# What Happens to Your Hotel's OTA Ranking When You Reply Slowly

A hotel ranked third in their market on Booking.com. By the time the general manager noticed Q4 bookings were tracking 38% below forecast, they had slipped to ninth. The rate hadn't changed. The photos were the same. No significant negative reviews had landed. What had changed was their message response time — a metric nobody was watching, sitting in a tab in the extranet that nobody had thought to check.

This is not an unusual pattern. According to [OTA ranking analysts at TravelScrape](https://www.travelscrape.com/ota-ranking-visibility.php), in 80% of cases where hotel clients report a drop in OTA bookings, the cause is a ranking problem, not a pricing one. And the most common driver of that ranking problem is response time.

---

## Is your reply time actually a ranking signal?

Yes — on Booking.com, response time is a direct algorithm input, not a soft quality indicator. The platform tracks a "reply score": the percentage of guest messages answered within 24 hours, calculated across a rolling 30-day window. [Properties that fall below a 90% response rate face ranking penalties](https://www.guestara.com/post/hotel-guest-message-response-time-benchmarks), and the platform does not send an alert when your score crosses the threshold. The algorithm simply adjusts your position, quietly.

The competitive standard among well-ranked properties is under one hour during operating hours. Ninety percent within 24 hours keeps you out of penalty territory — it does not make you competitive.

When we first access a property's Booking.com Extranet as part of an inbox audit, the reply score is almost never where the team thinks it is. Front desk staff gauge their responsiveness by feel — how quickly they check their phone, whether they got to messages before noon. The extranet number tells a different story. Properties that consider themselves responsive routinely sit at 78–84%, because the score is a 30-day rolling average that weights every overnight hour equally. One well-staffed day does not cancel out five unmanned nights.

Expedia tracks and displays response rate directly on your listing. While Expedia does not publish the precise algorithmic weight, lower response rates correlate with lower partner status and reduced placement in search results. Airbnb is the most explicit of the three: Superhost status requires a 90%+ response rate over 365 days, and [properties that respond within one hour see 25% more instant bookings](https://ensoconnect.com/resources/response-times-in-hospitality-a-guide-for-vacation-rental-professionals) according to platform data cited by Enso Connect. The shared logic across all three platforms is the same: a guest who sends a pre-booking question and waits hours does not wait — they move to the next property on the list.

---

## Why does a slow response hurt your ranking, not just one booking?

The algorithm penalises pattern, not individual incidents. A single missed reply in a strong month does not move your reply score enough to matter. But if your inbox is routinely unmanned overnight — the same gap, every night — your 30-day average reflects it consistently, and the ranking adjusts accordingly.

The consequence goes further than fewer eyes on your listing. It is that fewer people see your property during the weeks when they are still deciding. [OTAs captured 61% of bookings for independent properties in 2024, and 77% in Europe](https://www.d-edge.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Hotel-Distribution-Report-2024-EN.pdf), according to the D-Edge Hotel Distribution Report 2024. For most independent hotels, OTA visibility is not a secondary channel — it is the primary one. A ranking drop in October does not show in occupancy until December, after the booking window for that period has already closed.

There is a second penalty that compounds the first. A low reply score blocks you from achieving Booking.com Preferred Partner status — the tier that [gives properties up to 65% more page views and 40% more bookings](https://rentalsunited.com/blog/improve-booking-com-ranking/) compared to standard listings. Slow responses cost you two things at the same time: your position in algorithmic search results, and the Preferred Partner badge that would have amplified that position further.

The delay is what makes this particularly damaging. By the time your revenue numbers show the problem, the opportunity to fix it for that month is gone.

## What does "slow" actually mean on each platform?

Each OTA uses different language and different thresholds — but none of them are transparent about the exact algorithmic weight of response time.

| Platform | Minimum threshold | Competitive standard | What gets tracked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booking.com | 90% of messages answered within 24 hrs | Under 1 hour during operating hours | Reply score (rolling 30-day window) |
| Expedia | Not published | Under 1 hour recommended | Response rate (visible on listing, affects partner tier) |
| Airbnb | 90% over 365 days | Within 1 hour | Response rate + Superhost eligibility |

Booking.com is the most consequential for most independent hotels because the reply score is a live ranking variable, not a badge. The platform measures the percentage of messages responded to within 24 hours over the past 30 days. Miss the 90% threshold and your listing moves down — immediately, and with no notification.

The overnight gap is where most independent properties accumulate the damage. [The industry average response time to guest messages for short-term rental hosts is 9 minutes](https://ensoconnect.com/resources/response-times-in-hospitality-a-guide-for-vacation-rental-professionals). Hotel front desks typically have nobody monitoring OTA inboxes between 10pm and 7am — a nine-hour window in which pre-booking questions from guests in different time zones sit unanswered.

## Why do most hotels discover this too late?

The reply score lives in the Booking.com Extranet, not in your PMS or your front desk log. If your team is not checking the Messaging Analytics tab, you will not see the number move until it has already affected your position. Most front desk teams do not check it — because the extranet is primarily used for rate and availability management, because the metric is buried two clicks from the home screen, and because there is no push notification when it falls.

The feedback loop compounds the delay. Slow replies depress the reply score. A lower score reduces ranking and impressions. Fewer impressions means fewer enquiries. Fewer enquiries means lower occupancy — four to six weeks after the original response-time problem began. By the time a general manager flags underperformance in a monthly review, the cause is already a month old and the window for that period is closed.

The one thing that breaks this loop is checking the metric weekly. Go to Booking.com Extranet → Inbox → Messaging Analytics. Your reply score and average response time are both displayed there. If you have not looked at this in the past 30 days, you do not currently know whether you have a problem.

## Who is monitoring your OTA inbox at 11pm?

This is a staffing problem, not a technology problem. Auto-responses and template libraries can acknowledge that a message was received. They cannot answer a rate enquiry, confirm a specific room type's availability, or reassure a guest who is choosing between your property and two others at 11:45pm.

For most independent properties, evening front desk coverage handles check-in, guest requests, and in-person questions from roughly 3pm to 9pm. OTA pre-booking enquiries arrive during the same window — and the team responds to what is physically in front of them. After 10pm, the inbox is unmanned.

A guest searching from London at midnight — 7am in their destination's time zone — sends a pre-booking question to three shortlisted properties. The one that responds overnight takes the booking. The others respond at 8am and find the booking has already been made elsewhere.

This is not a training issue or a process gap. It is a coverage gap — the same kind the [Booking.com and Expedia Message Support Checklist](/resources/booking-com-message-support) is built to help you identify across your channels and hours.

## What does recovering a lost ranking actually look like?

Recovery is possible, but the mathematics work against you. Booking.com's 30-day rolling window means that rebuilding from 82% to above 90% requires four weeks of clean, consistent replies at full coverage. During those four weeks, the ranking stays suppressed and the booking impact continues.

Two distinct situations determine the path forward.

**Recoverable:** A staff transition, a holiday period, or an isolated short-staffed stretch caused the drop. Staffing is restored, reply rates are back above 90%, and you need the 30-day window to reset. Expect two to four weeks before ranking returns to its previous position.

**Structural gap:** The inbox is routinely unmanned overnight and at weekends. This is not an exception — it is the standard operating model. Response time will continue to fall unless the coverage model changes. Better templates, clearer SOPs, and team training will not close a nine-hour overnight gap.

The pattern holds across property types. A lean two-person front desk at a 30-room boutique operates identically to a 15-room inn in this respect: the inbox goes dark when the last staff member locks up. The size of the property does not determine the size of the gap — the staffing model does.

If the gap is structural, the only fix is adding coverage during the hours that are currently unmonitored.

## What should you check before making any decision?

Start with measurement, not action.

**Check your reply score today.** Log into Booking.com Extranet → Inbox → Messaging Analytics. Both your reply score and average response time are shown there. If you have not looked in the past 30 days, this is the starting point — not guessing at what might be wrong.

**Map your unmonitored hours.** Write down the hours in which nobody on your team is actively watching the OTA inbox. For most independent hotels this is a ten-hour overnight block. That is the exposure window.

**Set a triage rule for what matters most.** Pre-booking enquiries — any message that arrives before a reservation exists — need a response within 30 minutes during your operating hours. They are the messages that directly affect your reply score and your conversion rate. Post-booking operational questions (parking, check-in time, early arrival) can wait until morning without ranking penalty.

**Check the score weekly, not monthly.** The 30-day window punishes a run of consecutive bad nights faster than a monthly reporting cycle catches it.

If mapping your unmonitored hours reveals a gap of four hours or more per night, manual process changes are unlikely to close it. That is when coverage — not procedure — becomes the answer. ## When is the gap too large to close manually?

One trained person monitoring your OTA inboxes overnight, replying to pre-booking enquiries within 30 minutes, costs less than the revenue from a single recovered booking per week in almost every hotel market. The question is not whether the economics make sense — it is whether your operation can add that coverage role internally, or whether outsourcing is the more practical path.

Properties that outsource OTA inbox management to a dedicated team maintain reply scores in the 95–100% range consistently, because the overnight gap is the first thing a dedicated service closes. That consistent performance compounds into a ranking advantage over properties with structural overnight gaps — an advantage that shows in impressions, enquiries, and occupancy over the following months.

## Frequently asked questions

**How does response time affect my Booking.com ranking?**
Booking.com uses a "reply score" — the percentage of messages answered within 24 hours over the past 30 days — as a direct ranking input. Properties that fall below 90% face ranking penalties. The platform does not send a notification when your score crosses the threshold; the ranking simply adjusts.

**What is the minimum response rate required to avoid ranking penalties on Booking.com?**
The minimum threshold is 90% of messages answered within 24 hours. The competitive standard among well-ranked properties is under one hour during operating hours. Staying above 90% keeps you out of penalty territory — it does not make you competitive against properties that respond in under an hour.

**How long does it take for an OTA ranking to recover after a response time drop?**
Because Booking.com uses a 30-day rolling window, recovery requires four weeks of consistently high reply rates above the 90% threshold. During that window the ranking stays suppressed, and the booking impact continues.

**Does Expedia also use response time as a ranking factor?**
Expedia tracks and displays your response rate on your listing and factors it into partner status. While Expedia does not publish the exact algorithmic weight, lower response rates correlate with reduced visibility and lower partner tier placement.

**What hours do most hotel OTA inboxes go unmonitored?**
Most independent hotel operations do not actively monitor OTA inboxes between 10pm and 7am to 8am. That is a nine to ten hour overnight gap — the same window when guests in different time zones are most likely to send pre-booking enquiries and compare properties before confirming.

**Can auto-responses or templates protect my reply score?**
An auto-response can acknowledge receipt and stop the clock on Booking.com's reply timer in some configurations, but it cannot answer a rate question, confirm availability, or convert a comparison shopper. Templates protect the metric; they do not close the booking.
